The problem: paid for work nobody can fully verify

A pharmacist opens the on-call pharmacy on a Sunday. The collective agreement pays those hours more. Who proves them?

Every month, someone chases a salary already earned. The one who pays holds both the money and the proof.

Same shape, other jobs

  • a freelancer's delivery, judged against a spec
  • a sales commission, judged against a contract
  • a supplier's invoice, advanced before the debtor pays

What is missing

Not a promise. A place where the money sits that the payer can no longer empty or redirect, and a rule for who opens it.

The rule

The agent reads. The code computes. A human approves. Solana pays.

  • The AI agent gathers the evidence: the rota, the badge log, the Sunday hours, the invoice dossier. It writes a memo. It never moves money.
  • The code computes the amount line by line; anyone can replay it.
  • A human approves the statement with a signature. Nothing moves before it.
  • The program locks the money with one possible outcome, and on the date pays the one address set at creation. Not before. Not to anyone else.

How Solana powers it: a program with no owner

  • Solana has no native escrow, so the Anchor program is the escrow: the funds sit in a PDA nobody has the key to, in SOL or USDC.
  • Time-locked (payroll): no release before the date by the cluster clock, no cancel ever, not even by the employer; anyone may release after, only to the destination written at creation.
  • Hash-locked (delivery): only the secret releases, and the secret becomes public in that same call.
  • Several escrows in one transaction: all or nothing (net pay + contributions + tax).

Measured, not promised

  • 15 tests against a local validator, one per guarantee
  • every refusal replayed on devnet with its signature in the README: TooEarly, NotCancellable, WrongDestination…
  • the sealed payslip: a non-transferable NFT, readable by the named readers only
  • a shared vault, 2 of 3: the rental deposit, run with Phantom

A consumer app: one page, no terminal

  • Phantom, or a throwaway devnet key created in the browser
  • recipient, amount in dollars, date — lock
  • release, cancel, close, each with its explorer link; the refusals are shown as the program returns them
  • the payer chooses what to pay with: SOL, XRP or NEAR — Aurora quotes live
  • the fee per act is shown before the lock, and paid on top: what the recipient gets is never touched

The invoice advance: the program holds the ceilings

On the XRP Ledger, two signers out of three passed an advance above the 30 % concentration cap: the ledger knew no cap. On Solana the program holds it.
